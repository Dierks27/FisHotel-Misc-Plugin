Requests: a new section — a request sheet for the fish people ask for, hidden from the public at the start (no menu item, nothing in a sitemap, `noindex`; the form exists only behind a private link). Three tables through the autoloaded `fishotel_requests_db_version` gate (written only once they exist; a failed install is retried in wp-admin at most hourly): requests, private links, an append-only log. **Three ways in**: a *private link* made under Requests → Links (a label, an optional fee, expiry and maximum uses) opens a plain page that works logged out — name, e-mail, forum handle, then one or more fish with name suggestions from the species list and the published fish products, a standard size (free text when the Clean Room has none), a quantity, a note — behind a nonce, a honeypot and a rate limit per address and per e-mail; *by hand* under Requests → Add (approved as it is saved; "a replacement for a lost fish" makes it owed, never a fee); and `fishotel_requests_create()` for other sections (source `lost_fish`: owed, no fee, on the list at once; a WP_Error while the section is off). **Approve**: a sheet from a link waits under *Needs you* — Approve ticked, with or without a fee, or Decline (an e-mail only when ticked). **The fee** uses the store's wallet deposit, so the money lands in the customer's wallet through Batch Manager's own handling: one invoice may cover a whole sheet; the pay link is signed over the request and the amount (a changed amount is refused), puts Batch Manager's deposit product in the cart at that amount — that line only, quantity 1, never discounted — and tags the order line; when the order is paid the requests go on the list and the history says whether the wallet was credited. The pay link asks for a login and the checkout refuses a fee without an account, because the wallet credited is the paying account's. One reminder after 3 days, expiry after 7. Without Batch Manager fees are unavailable and every screen says so. **The list**: grouped by fish — "<fish> — 3 waiting · longest 4 months" — or as one list, with a *Needs you* strip (sheets to approve, people to tell, payments overdue, owed fish to send), filters by state, source and where it came from, a search over name, e-mail, handle and fish, sorting, and each request's own page with its whole history. **The state follows the fish** (Clean Room, behind `class_exists`): waiting → on order when a draft or ordered supplier order has a line of that fish and size (oldest request first, never more fish than the line orders) → in quarantine when the line is checked in → ready when the tank group's sale date comes → offered (the "ready" e-mail with the product link, by itself when Settings says so, else one click) → filled when the owner marks it or that customer buys that product; and back to waiting when the fish is not there after all. The owner can match and unmatch by hand; the supplier order page shows *Requested by: …* under each line through one new `fishotel_cleanroom_order_line` action. **Customers**: My Account → Requests and, without an account, a personal link in each e-mail — their own requests only, with Cancel. **Housekeeping**: after 4 months a "still want it?" e-mail whose Keep and Cancel links each need a click on the page; no answer in 14 days closes it. **Demand**: the most requested fish beside the quantities ordered on past supplier orders per year, and who has waited longest. **E-mail**: five kinds through `wp_mail()` as HTML with a plain-text part, each with a switch, subject and body in Settings, each logged. **Later**: *Open to the public* (off) enables `[fishotel_request_form]` for logged-in customers with a default fee and a cap of 5 open requests. An ordinary front-end request costs no query. Headless tests: rules, service, create, follow (against the Clean Room's own classes), fees, listing, front, admin, and two node tests.
